# Security.

Last updated 13 October 2026.

A signature is only worth the evidence behind it. This page describes how Sahihi produces that
evidence and protects it. Everything here describes how the product works today.

## Every file is fingerprinted

- When a PDF is uploaded, Sahihi records its **SHA-256** hash. The hash is checked again before
  anything is stamped onto it, and an original can't change once it's ready to send.
- Signers' values are stamped into the PDF **on our servers**, never in the browser. The finished
  file is hashed again, and both hashes go on the Certificate of Completion.
- Supporting files are hashed too, limited to common document and image types, and always
  downloaded rather than opened in the page.

## A record nobody can quietly rewrite

- Every step of an envelope (sent, viewed, code verified, signed, declined, voided, completed) is
  written to an **audit trail**. Each entry includes the hash of the one before, so a changed or
  missing entry breaks the chain.
- The database itself refuses to update or delete audit entries: the application's database role
  has no permission to, and a trigger blocks updates from anyone, administrators included.
- The latest hash of the chain is printed on the certificate, so a later rewrite of the history
  would no longer match it.

## Anyone can check a document

- Each certificate carries a code and a QR code that open the public verify page.
- Anyone can also drop a PDF onto the verify page. The file is **hashed in their browser** and only
  the fingerprint is sent. It matches signed documents and certificates, never unsigned originals.
- The verify page shows signers with their email addresses masked, and nothing about other
  envelopes.

## Signing links and codes

- Each signer gets a unique link made from 32 random bytes. Sahihi stores **only a hash** of it;
  the link itself exists in the email and nowhere else. It expires with the envelope, changes with
  every reminder, and stops working if the envelope is voided.
- You can ask signers for a **six-digit code** by email or SMS first. Codes are stored as hashes,
  expire after 10 minutes and allow 5 attempts.
- Signing pages aren't indexed by search engines and don't pass the link on to other sites.
- Public pages are rate-limited.

## Workspaces stay separate

- Every document, envelope and template belongs to one workspace, and every request is checked
  against it. An automated test calls every route as a member of another workspace and expects
  nothing back.
- Files sit in private storage and are reached only through short-lived signed links.
- Roles decide who can send, manage templates, invite people or change workspace settings.
- Members sign in with a password, a passkey or an authenticator app (two-factor).

## Integrations

- API keys carry only the scopes you give them.
- Webhooks are delivered only to public `https://` addresses and are signed with HMAC-SHA256, so
  your server can check they came from Sahihi. Webhook secrets are stored encrypted and shown once.
- Embedded signing only works on the website origins you allow.

## Your own storage

- On Business, completed envelopes are copied to storage you connect: an S3-compatible bucket
  (Amazon S3, Cloudflare R2, Backblaze B2 and others), Google Drive or OneDrive. On Enterprise,
  every file of the workspace can live in your own bucket instead of ours.
- Sahihi never deletes anything in your storage. It asks only for permission to write, read and
  list, so your bucket's own permissions guarantee it. Files Sahihi stops using are listed for you
  to clean up.
- Your browser never talks to your bucket: files are served through short-lived links on Sahihi's
  own address, so your bucket needs no public access and its address stays private.
- Storage keys and drive sign-ins are stored encrypted and never shown again. On Amazon S3 you can
  use an IAM role instead of keys, tied to your workspace with its own external ID.
- Uploads are checked before they reach your bucket, and the fingerprints stay with Sahihi, so a
  file changed in your storage no longer verifies.

## Your data, on your schedule

- Owners and admins choose how long completed envelopes are kept, and can export the workspace's
  data at any time.
- Deleting an envelope removes its files and the signers' personal data, but keeps the hashes and
  the audit trail, so its certificate still verifies. Files in storage you connected stay there
  until you remove them.
- The drafting assistant is off until an owner or admin turns it on, because document text is sent
  to the model provider while it's on.
- Logs mask signing links and personal details before they are written.

## What's next

- Signatures backed by a licensed Certification Authority (PAdES), alongside the certificate.
- Regular anchoring of audit-chain hashes with an independent timestamp authority.
- Malware scanning for supporting files.

## Reporting a problem

If you think you've found a security issue, email
[security@sahihi.co.ke](mailto:security@sahihi.co.ke). Please give us a chance to fix it before
you share it publicly.
